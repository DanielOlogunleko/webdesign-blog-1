Reading Assignment

16 February 2026

Overview

This week’s readings helped me understand that the Internet is not just something abstract or wireless, but actually a physical system made up of cables, infrastructure, and labor.

The Undersea Network (Introduction Chapter) - Nicole Starosielski, 2015

This reading was probably the most shocking to me because I learned that most of the Internet actually travels through cables under the ocean, not satellites. The introduction explains that undersea cables carry almost all transoceanic communication, including messages, videos, and websites.

Before this, I always imagined satellites being responsible for global communication. Learning that cables on the ocean floor connect continents made the Internet feel more real and also more fragile. It made me realize that something as simple as sending a message to someone in another country depends on physical cables deep underwater.

This reading also made me think about power and geography. The placement of these cables affects which countries have faster or better Internet access. It made me realize that the Internet is not equally accessible to everyone.

Overall, this reading made me see the Internet as a physical and global system, not just something on my screen. It made me realize how connected the world is, but also how dependent we are on infrastructure we never see.

Networks of New York – Ingrid Burrington

Reading Networks of New York completely changed how I see the Internet in my everyday life. Before this reading, I honestly never thought about where the Internet physically exists. I just assumed it was something wireless, like Wi-Fi floating in the air. But this reading made it clear that the Internet is actually made up of physical cables and infrastructure hidden under the streets. For example, the author explains that Internet cables run through underground ducts owned by companies like Verizon and Empire City Subway, and most of us walk over them without even realizing it.

What really stood out to me was how invisible this infrastructure is, even though it’s everywhere. The reading made me realize that the Internet is not some abstract “cloud,” but something physical and controlled by companies. It also made me think about how much we rely on something we barely understand. Every day, I use my phone and laptop constantly, but I never stop to think about the systems making it possible.

This reading made me more aware of my surroundings. Now, I feel like when I walk around New York, I’ll start noticing things like manhole covers, cable markings, and infrastructure that I used to ignore. It made me realize that the city is not just buildings and streets, but also hidden networks that keep everything connected.

The World of Hidden Technology Underground — The Atlantic

This reading helped me understand how much human labor goes into maintaining the Internet and other underground infrastructure. Before this, I never thought about the people whose job it is to locate cables underground. It made me realize that technology doesn’t just exist on its own people have to manage and maintain it constantly.

What stood out to me the most was how fragile and complex these underground systems are. One mistake while digging could damage important cables and cause serious disruptions. It also surprised me that even with modern technology, people still rely on physical tools and experience to find these cables.

This reading made me appreciate the hidden work behind the technology I use every day. It also made me realize that the Internet isn’t as simple or automatic as it seems. There’s an entire physical and human system supporting it behind the scenes.
