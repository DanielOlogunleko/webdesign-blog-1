Reading Response:

Article 1 - Heavy lies the Digital Cloud

My thoughts on this article which talks about how the cloud is becoming like an external brain for humans. Example would be instead of people remembering things ourselves like photos, notes, conversations. etc we store them online. The author arugues that “The Cloud” has become a footprint that include our most private thoughts "In a recent OpenAI study, 11% of usage fell under “expressing,” using ChatGPT for reflection, exploration, and even, yes, confession. Our archives are no longer just photos and playlists, but diaries poured into machine prompts." What stood out to me was the idea that our memories are tied to identity and if our memories are stored in the cloud, then part of who we are are stored there which is unsettling because we don't fully control those systems.

Article 2 - CHATGPT is a Blurry JPEG of the Web

Ted Chiang explains how ChatGPT works by comparing it to a “blurry JPEG.” At first, that just sounds like a simple metaphor, but it actually means something. What he means is that AI doesn’t store or understand information the way humans do. Instead, it compresses huge amounts of text into patterns and then reconstructs responses that are close enough to sound correct. The important part is that this process is "lossy", meaning some information is lost along the way. That’s why ChatGPT can give answers that sound fluent and intelligent but are slightly off or completely wrong. Chiang gives the example of a copied floor plan where the numbers were changed but still looked believable. That stuck with me because it shows that the danger isn’t obvious mistakes it’s subtle ones that you might not catch.

Article 3 - Frank Chimero: The Web's Grain

In The Web’s Grain, Frank Chimero argues that the web is not a blank canvas but a medium with its own structure, behaviors, and limitations. He compares the web to a material with a “grain,” meaning it naturally works in certain ways such as stacking content vertically, adapting to different screen sizes, and allowing continuous scrolling. Because of this, designers should not try to force the web to act like print or other fixed formats.

This article also explains that when designers ignore how the web naturally functions, the result can feel awkward or difficult to use. Instead, good web design comes from working with the web’s flexibility and responsiveness. Also highlighted was how the web is constantly expanding and evolving, which makes it less about controlling every detail and more about organizing content in a way that works across different devices and contexts. Overall, the main idea is that understanding the “nature” of the web leads to better design, while trying to fight against it leads to poor user experiences.

Reflection:

These readings made me more aware of how I approach my work in class. Instead of just trying to make something look good or function quickly, I should focus more on understanding the tools and systems I’m using. Whether it’s AI, the web, or digital storage, I need to be more intentional and not just rely on convenience. I think that mindset will help me not just in this class, but in any future projects I work on.
