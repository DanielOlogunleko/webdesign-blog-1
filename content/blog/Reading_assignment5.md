# “The Internet Has Borders” - Anna Rose Kerr, 2025

What I took from this article is that the internet is not as open or neutral as it seems. Before reading it, I thought of the internet as something global and free, but the author explains that it’s actually shaped by physical infrastructure, governments, and corporations. For example, data isn’t just floating in the “cloud”it’s stored in real places and controlled by specific countries and companies.

One thing that stood out to me was the idea that even choosing where to host a website can be a political decision. The author talks about how their own website exists across different countries, showing how complicated and interconnected the system really is. This made me realize that technology isn’t neutral—it reflects power and global inequalities.

Overall, this article changed how I think about the internet. It’s not just a tool we use; it’s a system shaped by politics, economics, and control. That means every decision we make online, even something small, is part of a bigger structure.

# “Alive Internet Theory” - Spencer Chang, 2025

This article gave a different, more hopeful perspective on the internet. While a lot of people believe in the “dead internet theory” (that bots and AI are taking over), the author argues that there is still real human life online.

What stood out to me is how the author talks about smaller, more genuine spaces on the internet—like forums, group chats, and community projects. Even though big platforms focus on engagement and profit, people still find ways to connect in meaningful ways. That made me think about how I personally use the internet, and how some of my best interactions don’t come from major social media apps but from smaller communities.

It compares the internet to a city that has been overdeveloped, which I thought was a strong image. It shows how the internet has become more controlled and less human, but at the same time, it suggests that we can still reclaim it. Overall, I took away that the internet isn’t “dead” it’s just evolving, and it’s up to users to keep it alive.

# “I Know a Place” - Everest Pipkin, 2021

What I took from this article is how the internet can feel both empty and meaningful at the same time. The author talks about exploring Roblox worlds, especially abandoned or unused ones, and finding beauty in them.

One thing that stood out to me was the idea that even empty digital spaces still represent human creativity. Someone took the time to build those worlds, even if no one is there anymore. That made me think about how the internet is full of forgotten content, but it still holds meaning because it reflects people’s experiences and imagination.

I also liked how the author compared these digital spaces to real-life places where people hang out, like parks or neighborhoods. It shows that the internet isn’t just about consuming content—it can also be a place to exist, explore, and create.

Overall, this article made me see the internet in a more emotional and creative way. Even in spaces that seem empty, there’s still evidence of human presence, which makes the internet feel more alive than it might appear.
