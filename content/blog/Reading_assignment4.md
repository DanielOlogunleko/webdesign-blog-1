# Reading Response:


# Article 1 – The Woman Who Taught Internet Strangers to Actually Care for One Another

What I think this article is about is; it centered around the idea that the internet used to be built around smaller communities instead of giant platforms. Stacy Horn’s online community, Echo, worked because people actually knew each other and moderators cared about maintaining the space. In today's world, social media companies have billions of users, which makes that kind of care almost impossible. This article argues that modern platforms hide the labor of moderation and treat it like invisible work instead of something important.
One part that I found interesting was how moderators on Echo were regular members of the community rather than outsourced workers. Which is very different from platforms like Facebook or Instagram today, where moderation is often done by people who have to constantly look at disturbing content. It made me realize that the internet became less human as it became more profitable. 

# Article 2 – Another Network is Possible

This article argues that the internet does not have to be controlled by large corporations. Indymedia was an example of a network where activists created their own spaces to share information, organize, and support local communities. What stood out to me was that Indymedia was not just online. People also made newspapers, radio stations, and physical meeting spaces.
I think that detail matters because today most people think posting online is the same thing as organizing, but this article argues that real activism still requires in-person connection. Social media can make people feel involved because they repost something or make a viral tweet, but that does not always create lasting change. I think the article is really criticizing how social media turns activism into content. It made me think about how often people are more focused on visibility than actual action.

# Article 3 – Architecting Emergence

This article was more abstract than the other two, but I think it connects to them because it is also about how systems shape people’s behavior. The authors argue that algorithms often reinforce the same patterns instead of allowing creativity or change. For example, hiring systems may continue to favor the same kinds of people because they are trained on biased information from the past.

What stood out to me was the idea of “emergence,” where something new is created when many people contribute together. The ThingThingThing project is interesting because nobody fully controls it, yet it still becomes a functioning world. Compared to the other articles, this one feels more optimistic because it imagines technology being used to encourage creativity and collaboration rather than control.

# Reflection

I think that all three readings are really talking about the same problem which is that the modern internet has become too focused on profit, scale, and efficiency. Because of that, people have lost some of the care, creativity, and sense of community that used to exist online.

The first article shows how smaller communities were better at making people feel supported. The second article showed how independent networks can help people organize outside of corporate platforms. The third article imagines what technology could look like if it encouraged collaboration instead of control.

These readings connect to class because they all raise questions about how digital spaces should be designed.  I think it is important to remember that technology is not neutral because the choices people make when building websites, platforms, and algorithms affect how users behave and how communities form.
